Real Environments. Real Preparation.

FIELD REALITY

The job doesn't always happen in a clean body shop with good lighting and a cooperative customer. You'll know that going in.

Body Shop

Working Around Active Repairs

Shops are busy. Vehicles are partially disassembled. Estimators may want to talk while you're working. You'll train how to inspect efficiently, document what you need, and maintain professionalism without slowing down the shop's operation.

Tow Yard

Limited Access, Full Documentation

Vehicles may be stacked, muddy, or locked. You can't always get the angle you want. We cover the adapted inspection protocol — what to photograph when access is limited, how to note what you couldn't reach, and when to request better access before submitting.

Customer Driveway

The Policyholder Is Watching

Some inspections happen at the owner's home with them standing five feet away. You'll know exactly what to say on arrival, how to manage expectations during the walk-around, and how to close the interaction cleanly without overpromising anything.

Remote / Desk

Working Off Photos and Notes Alone

Desk review assignments come with a photo set and a basic damage description. No vehicle in front of you. You'll train how to evaluate remote documentation, identify what's missing, and write a defensible review from what you actually have.

THE POINT
None of these environments will surprise you. Other programs prepare you for the textbook version of this job. We prepare you for the version that actually exists — where conditions are imperfect, people are impatient, and you still need to submit a clean, complete, defensible claim package.
